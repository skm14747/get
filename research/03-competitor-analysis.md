# Competitor analysis

**Accessed:** 2026-10-24, except where a source has its own publication date.  
**Not available:** domain authority, referring domains, Ahrefs or Semrush traffic, and Google’s top 10. A Google search for “merge pdf” returned Google’s automated “sorry” interstitial, so live Google results were not inspected.

Named competitors from the brief that were **not** opened, and are therefore not scored: RapidTables, Online-Convert, Convertio, UnitConverters.net, Calculator Soup, and RapidAPI. Relevance is plausible and unverified.

## What was checked

### iLovePDF

- Compress page: https://www.ilovepdf.com/compress_pdf
- FAQ: https://www.ilovepdf.com/help/faq
- Documentation: https://www.ilovepdf.com/help/documentation
- Privacy: https://www.ilovepdf.com/help/privacy

The compress tool offers extreme, recommended, and less compression, and the documentation says higher compression means lower file size and quality. Files can be chosen from the device, Google Drive, or Dropbox. The FAQ says files are kept for a maximum of two hours and then removed, transfers use HTTPS, and a password entered to open a protected file is not stored. The privacy policy says ILOVEPDF S.L. in Barcelona acts as processor of file contents and does not use that content to train AI models.

Autocomplete, not a rank check, associates iLovePDF with PDF head terms in the US, France, French-speaking Canada, Spain, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, and Kuwait.

### Smallpdf

- https://www.smallpdf.com/compress-pdf

The page describes a free compressor, Basic versus Moderate and Strong levels, and says Moderate and Strong are Pro features. It claims deletion after one hour, TLS, GDPR compliance, and ISO/IEC 27001. “Browser-based” appears alongside language about file transfer, so this is not a local-only tool on the evidence of that page. “Trusted by over 1 billion users since 2013” is the site’s own claim and was not audited.

### PDF24

- https://tools.pdf24.org/en/compress-pdf

The page says compression reduces image resolution and quality, exposes DPI and image-quality controls, and states: “PDF files are compressed in the cloud on our servers.” Files are deleted after a short period. The page also says “No limits,” which is a product claim, not a measurement. This is the feature bar a structural pdf-lib rewrite does not meet.

### timeanddate.com

- https://www.timeanddate.com/worldclock/converter.html

The converter says it accounts for daylight saving time and accepts present, past, or future dates. The page links a Time API. This is a specialist, not a thin utility. No weakness was observed in the retrieved copy. Mobile speed, ads, and backlinks were not measured.

### Calculator.net

- Percentage: https://www.calculator.net/percent-calculator.html
- Sales tax: https://www.calculator.net/sales-tax-calculator.html
- VAT: https://www.calculator.net/vat-calculator.html

The percentage page includes the ordinary formulas and worked examples. The VAT and sales-tax pages explain the taxes and calculate from rates the user supplies. A country-preset directory was not observed. That is a localization gap relative to “VAT calculator UK” or “calcul TVA Belgique,” not evidence that Calculator.net ranks poorly.

### Adobe

Adobe Acrobat was not reviewed as a product. “Adobe” appears in autocomplete for many PDF operations and in the PDF24/iLovePDF competitive set. No Adobe page was scored.

## Bing note

Bing was queried as a fallback. The session was not reliable enough to use as a ranking dataset. “merge pdf” returned unrelated film results. “word counter,” “json formatter,” and “unix timestamp converter” drifted to Microsoft Word, MDN, and Unix as an operating system. One query, “compress pdf,” returned recognizable tool titles for iLovePDF, Smallpdf, PDF24, Adobe, pdfgear.com, and PDF2GO. That single list is a weak corroboration of the brand set, not a Google top 10.

## Opportunity these gaps do support

A new site can be truthful about local processing, skip accounts, and preset a tax rate only when it cites the authority and the date. Those are product differences. They are not proof that Google will rank the pages.

A new site should not describe a pdf-lib rewrite as a way to reach 200KB. PDF24 already tells users that image resolution is the main lever, and that lossless compression does little.
